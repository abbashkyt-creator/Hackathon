# Three-Minute Winning Demo

## 0:00–0:20 — Hook

Open the QR code on a phone. Do not explain a menu because there is none.

> “Short-form feeds removed every decision before entertainment. We kept that zero-friction loop and replaced passive watching with play.”

Tap Pulse Lock immediately.

## 0:20–0:55 — Prove the loop

Land two pulses, miss one, and let the result appear.

> “The game was already running. One thumb, one rule, no tutorial, no download.”

Point out the real score, percentile, rank and rival target.

## 0:55–1:25 — Prove competition

Open the leaderboard directly over the card. Switch from All time to Today.

> “The score is stored and ranked the moment the run ends. This board contains only real runs; we do not seed fake competitors.”

Tap Share and send/open the canonical challenge link on a second device.

## 1:25–2:05 — Prove variety and engineering

Swipe through Color Clash, Stack Shift, Memory Grid and Meteor Dodge.

> “Each swipe is a different mechanic, and only the visible game is alive. Leaving a card hard-stops its animation and audio.”

Briefly play the most visually reliable second game—Stack Shift.

## 2:05–2:35 — Prove identity without friction

Open the profile after a worthwhile score and claim it with Discord.

> “We earn the login. Guest play comes first, and the run merges into the account instead of disappearing.”

Refresh and show the personal best remains.

## 2:35–3:00 — Close

> “TikTok proved the feed is the fastest way to discover content. Tip Tap makes every card something you do. Land. Play. Compete. Swipe.”

End on the active game, not a slide.

## Pre-demo preparation

- Rehearse on the same deployed URL and Wi-Fi.
- Create at least five real leaderboard entries from the team.
- Verify the Discord callback after the final domain is known.
- Open the second-device challenge before going on stage.
- Keep a screen recording only as a network-failure backup.
- Never describe plausibility checks as “unhackable.”
